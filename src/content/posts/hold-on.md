---
title: "衣不如新，人不如故：固守的力量"
description: "A reflection on relentless innovation and delusional progress, and the vital need for deep cultural roots."
date: "2026-09-30"
---

People must spend so that firms can produce. Investors reap the returns to keep the money machine turning. This drives the K-shaped economy where the rich get richer and the rest get normalized—living but not thriving.

The trick we use here is innovation. Without it, there would be no reason to buy the next iPhone, the next EV, the next concert ticket, the next fashion trend, the new bag, or the fresh pair of shoes. In relative terms, any firm that fails to innovate is eventually squeezed out of the market.

All of this is framed as progress. We are told that through endless iteration, something better will inevitably evolve, and through relentless competition, the superior product will win.

This is how modern human civilization started, evolved, and continues to churn. It is baked deep into our collective DNA and is part of human nature itself, as fundamental as eating and sleeping.

Yet, there must be a balance: the need to stay grounded with deep roots. For a hyper-optimized system cannot withstand risk and will collapse when conditions shift, and a flourishing crown is no cure for a rotten root when stormy weather arrives.

As the ancient wisdom reminds us:

君子务本，本立而道生

In a stable system, innovation is the forward force while tradition is the restoring force. When the two work in tandem, progress is steady and enduring. Hollow progress driven by relentless innovation that abandons cultural roots is a recipe for extinction.

Are we doomed for extinction? Many sense the answer but keep quiet, while the noisy crowd shouts, "Forward! Forward!"

As Laozi warned:

企者不立，跨者不行

Those who truly understand simply keep the two in balance, waiting and ready for the day the entire structure collapses all at once.
