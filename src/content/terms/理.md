## 1. Etymological Roots and Evolution

Originally, **理** (*lǐ*) referred to the natural veins, grain, or markings found inside raw jade or wood—implying an inherent, organic structure rather than a human-imposed design. 

During the Han dynasty, it was used broadly to mean order or organization. However, it underwent a profound metaphysical transformation during the Song dynasty under the Cheng-Zhu school, where it became the cornerstone of Neo-Confucian thought. *Li* came to represent the fundamental, immutable principle underlying all things in the universe.

## 2. Key Dimensions

### Cosmic Pattern and Moral Law (*万物皆有理*)
For Neo-Confucians like Zhu Xi, every physical entity, plant, animal, and human mind possesses its own specific *Li*. This principle is both physical and moral: it is the reason why water flows downward, why fire burns upward, and why human beings possess an innate moral compass (*Xing* / 性).

### The Inseparability of Principle and Vital Force (*理气不相离*)
*Li* does not exist in a vacuum; it operates through **Qi** (*qì* — vital force/matter-energy). While *Li* provides the form, pattern, and normative blueprint, *Qi* provides the raw material substance. Nothing exists without both.

### Investigating Things to Reach Principle (*格物致知*)
Because *Li* is embedded in all things, human understanding is achieved through the active investigation of things (*Ge Wu* / 格物). By examining external phenomena and internal human affairs, one gradually comes to perceive the coherent thread of universal principle running through everything.
