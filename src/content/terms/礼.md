## 1. Core Meaning and Significance

In classical Chinese thought, **礼** (*Lǐ*) extends far beyond mere etiquette or religious ceremony. Originally referring to sacred sacrificial rites performed to honor spirits and ancestors, it was radically reinterpreted by Confucius (*Kongzi*) as the total framework of normative behavior that structures a civilized society. 

*Lǐ* provides the outward forms through which inner humaneness (**仁** / *Rén*) is expressed, cultivated, and made socially operational. Without *Lǐ*, inner virtue lacks direction and expression; without *Rén*, *Lǐ* becomes empty, rigid formalism.

---

## 2. Key Dimensions

### Social Order and Role Ethics (*名分*)
*Lǐ* defines the proper expectations, duties, and relational boundaries for individuals based on their social roles (ruler and subject, parent and child, elder and younger sibling). By establishing clear behavioral boundaries, it maintains social harmony and mutual respect.

### Refinement of Human Nature (*文质*)
Confucius emphasized the balance between substance (*质*) and refinement (*文*). While innate sincerity is essential, human impulses require the shaping influence of ritual discipline to transform raw emotional drives into cultivated, moral action.

### Alignment with Cosmic Order
In classical texts like the *Liji* (Book of Rites), *Lǐ* is not merely a human convention but a reflection of the orderly patterns of heaven and earth. Practicing ritual propriety aligns human society with the rhythm of the cosmos.
